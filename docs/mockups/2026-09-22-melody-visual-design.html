Melody — visual design

Open the clickable prototype (older, four columns)

The one visual design reference for Melody, desktop first: the ink language, type and icons, every button, component, chat widget and pane, the desktop window and its width budget, Melody's surfaces next to a normal session, motion and hover, tokens, words and the decisions still open. Drawn from theme-tokens.ts and DESIGN.md; where a rule changes, an amber-dotted Proposal says so. The theme switch redraws everything. Nothing here is built yet. Consolidated 2026-09-22 from the design reference and the components sheet, which it replaces.

0 · Ink language — the black-and-white mark sets the tone; teal is the accent, amber waits on you; green and red report outcomes

The app icon is the source: the #101010 Takes strip on a white rounded square. The chrome is ink on paper, and colour is earned. Teal is the accent: where you are and what's alive. Amber means something is waiting on you. Blue is kept for links and info notes only. Two service colours report outcomes: green for done, red for failed. Everything else is ink, paper and the greys between them.

The mark is always the icon in miniature, the same white tile on paper and on charcoal. 28 px on the pin, 16 px in a row, 112 px here

4 · The desktop window — three panels; Work is two tabbed panels

Sessions · Chat · Work, edge to edge with no gaps; a hairline divides them. Work is two tabbed panels: the top one, whose bar ends in + and the session menu ⋯, and a secondary bottom one, whose bar ends in +. Melody is a tab, in the bottom panel by default. The rules: when the bottom panel has no tabs, the top one extends down; when neither has tabs, Work collapses and Chat takes the width. The button at Chat's top right shows or hides Work; pressed when Work has no tabs, it opens a new Terminal. The pin (or ⌘J) opens or closes Melody's tab.

Try it in the window: click a tab, its × to close it, + for a terminal, the button at Chat's top right, the pin (or ⌘J) for Melody

5 · Width budget — three panels, who gives way

Chat keeps at least 480 px while the Work column shows, and its text keeps a 760 px measure however wide the window gets. Proposal for wide screens: from 1920 up, Work grows to a quarter of the window, capped at 960 (640 at 2560, 960 at 3840), so a pane and Melody get room instead of the chat getting empty margins. When there isn't room: 1 Work narrows from 480 to 360; 2 Sessions folds to its 56 px dot strip, and Work widens again if that frees room. Melody takes whatever width Work has. Panels meet edge to edge, divided by a hairline, so no width goes to gaps.

Proposal — amends the One Dock Rule and the per-panel tab bars (DESIGN.md §Principles, §Vocabulary). The frame stays three panels. Work holds up to two tabbed panels, top and bottom; the bottom one is secondary. Melody is a tab like any other, except that closing it only hides her: her conversation is kept, and the pin or ⌘J brings the tab back in the bottom panel. An empty bottom panel lets the top one extend down, and no tabs anywhere collapses Work. The Work toggle moves from the tab bar to Chat's top right, so it's reachable while Work is collapsed; the session menu ⋯ stays on the first bar, and right-clicking the session's row reaches it while Work is collapsed. The table is computed by the same function that draws §4.

6 · Melody pin — top of the Sessions column, above search

Clicking the pin opens or closes Melody's tab in Work's bottom panel (⌘J does the same). Her three friends sit under it as compact rows (§7). It's a Floating Button, and its second line always says what she's doing.

7 · Session rows — the C3 list, with a status dot and who started it

The sidebar stays C3 (DESIGN.md §Vocabulary, Sessions column): search, repository chips, groups by day, tabs at the foot. Each row gains two things: a status dot at the left with its word in the tooltip and accessible name, and in the meta line the mark of the friend who manages it (Harmony, Tempo, Chord), or none for a session you started. Melody and her friends aren't in the list; their pins above it are their rows, and clicking a friend opens their conversation and filters the list to their repository. When the column folds, it becomes the strip on the right.

Status colours follow the ink language (§0), and the Board and worker rows move with them: Running teal, with the 1600 ms halo · Needs you amber · Done green · Failed red, which stays · Idle an empty ring · the unread dot teal (§0). The word is never left out: the tooltip, the accessible name and the session header all say it.

8 · Melody

She's a tab in Work, in the bottom panel by default. You can drag her to the top one like any tab. On the inspector ground, her replies are flat in the body face, and your messages sit on the tint, as in chat. Under her tab, her line counts sessions and what needs you. Closing her tab hides her; she keeps working and counting on her pin. The specimens below are her tab's body at its default size.

9 · Session cards — Melody's link to a session

Whenever she mentions a session, her message ends in its card. The card is the same object as the rail row: dot, title, repository and status word. Clicking it opens the session in Chat, and right-clicking gives the session menu (§15). A card's status is its outline: 3 px, in its status colour (teal Running, amber Needs you, green Done, red Failed; Idle has none), with no dot inside. Rows, the strip and the header keep their top-left dot, so nothing carries both.

10 · Composer — attach · send, in Melody and in every Easy session

The lever goes, and the folder chip moves to the session header (§11). In Easy the row is just the paper clip and the send disc: Heroicons 16 solid, 16 px on buttons. Advanced keeps its chips as they are. The disc keeps its usage ring and its states.

Proposal — retires the lever. Lever.tsx, the words lever and stop, and the Easy row "lever · folder · attach · send" (DESIGN.md §Vocabulary) leave Easy. A new Easy session starts in the orchestrator setup and falls back to direct Opus in a folder with no orchestrator role. The research doc settles how that happens; this sheet only draws the row.

11 · Session view — header, question card, and Melody's handoff

The header now carries what the composer used to: the repository (the folder chip's job), the title, who started the session and its status. A session Melody started opens with a divider naming the handoff, the "→ Codex from here" pattern applied to people.

12 · Chat widgets

What a session posts in its thread. The rules: a widget never moves; it's posted once and updated in place. Running is the one lifted card; everything else floats and settles back. The fix is the primary. Status on a card is its 3 px outline. Left: a turn as it runs. Right: the widgets that stop and wait, or fail.
